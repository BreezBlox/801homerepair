# Logistics Rules

## Standing logistics rule

When materials are customer pickup:
- always verify the exact store location
- always verify real-time availability before telling the customer where to go
- do not rely on a broad product search alone
- do not send the customer to the wrong store, a far-away store, or a maybe-in-stock situation without saying so

This rule exists because a customer was previously sent to a store location where the item was not actually available there.
That is now a process failure to avoid, not a random oopsie.

## Pre-job tool / logistics audit

Before finalizing an estimate, shopping plan, or job plan, check:

1. **Tools already owned**
   - core tools on hand
   - battery status / charger status
   - blades, bits, consumables, and extension cords

2. **Specialty tools required**
   - digging tools
   - post-setting or post-pulling tools
   - saws or specialty blades
   - leveling / layout tools
   - demolition tools
   - anything rented vs bought

3. **On-site power reality**
   - power available or not
   - distance to outlets
   - extension cord needs
   - whether generator / inverter planning is needed

4. **Material handling**
   - who is buying materials
   - pickup store and stock confirmation
   - vehicle / transport limits
   - cut-to-fit in store vs on site
   - long-item transport constraints

5. **Access constraints**
   - gated access
   - stairs / upper floor
   - narrow entries
   - HOA constraints
   - noise / work-hour limits
   - parking distance

6. **Disposal / cleanup**
   - carpet haul-off
   - demolition debris
   - dump runs
   - bagging / containment needs
   - whether disposal is included, excluded, or conditional

7. **Jobsite conditions**
   - weather exposure for exterior work
   - slope / uneven grade
   - hidden damage risk
   - moisture issues
   - trip hazards and furniture moving needs

## Digging / excavation rule

If the job involves digging, post resets, fence repairs, or anything that breaks ground:
- do not assume the area is clear
- flag utility-locate / underground obstruction risk
- identify root issues, old concrete, buried hardware, irrigation, and unknown underground junk as possible scope multipliers
- avoid presenting the job as simple unless those risks have been checked

## Planning outputs

A good job plan should usually include:
- order of operations
- tool list
- material list
- sourcing / pickup notes
- hidden-risk notes
- what could change labor time or price

## Recommendation style

Recommendations should be realistic for field use.
That means:
- fewer fantasy-perfect plans
- more acknowledgment of what can actually go wrong on site
- more attention to transport, stock, setup time, and cleanup
