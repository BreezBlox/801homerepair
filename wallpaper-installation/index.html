Wallpaper 101

A beginner’s guide to choosing, measuring, and planning

Wallpaper is not one-size-fits-all. The backing, application method, pattern, and wall condition affect both the material count and installation plan. Use this as a starting point; the wallpaper and adhesive instructions for your specific products take priority.

1. Know what kind of wallpaper you have

Before buying paste or planning the job, find the product name or label and confirm its required application method.

Paste-the-wall

Apply compatible adhesive to the wall, but only when the paper is designed for this method.

Paste-the-paper

Apply adhesive to the paper. Some products need “booking” time before hanging; follow the label.

Pre-pasted

The backing has adhesive that must be activated as directed, usually with water or an approved activator.

Peel-and-stick

Self-adhesive paper needs a clean, dry, smooth, compatible surface. Texture and fresh paint can affect adhesion.

Murals are usually panel-based. Check the panel order, finished dimensions, and supplier’s measuring tool rather than treating a mural like a standard roll.

2. Measure each wall carefully

  • Sketch the room and label each wall. Measure each one separately.
  • Measure width and height in more than one place; note the largest dimensions if walls or ceilings vary.
  • Write down doors, windows, corners, outlets, fixtures, built-ins, and anything that affects access.
  • Do not subtract every opening automatically. A window may interrupt a strip without eliminating a full strip or its pattern waste.
  • For murals, use the maker’s directions for maximum wall width and height. Some made-to-measure suppliers include their own fitting margin.

3. Estimate rolls by strips—not area alone

  1. Divide each wall’s width by the wallpaper’s usable width and round up to get the number of vertical strips.
  2. Set each strip length from the wall height plus trimming allowance. Pattern repeats may require longer cuts.
  3. Divide the product’s roll length by the cut length and round down to find full strips per roll.
  4. Divide total strips by usable strips per roll and round up. Confirm how the product is packaged and sold.
Simple example—no pattern repeat: A 10-foot-wide by 8-foot-high wall needs six 20-inch-wide strips. With 2 inches extra at the top and bottom, each cut is about 100 inches. A 33-foot roll yields three full cuts, so plan for about two rolls’ worth before corner allowances, defects, or packaging differences.

4. Pattern repeats change the math

A pattern repeat is the distance before the design starts again. A match type tells you how neighboring strips line up—commonly random, straight, or drop match. Larger repeats and offset matches can create substantially more offcuts, so a square-foot estimate may undercount what you need.

Check the repeat and match on the product listing, then use the maker’s roll calculator when available. Buy enough from the same dye lot or run when possible, and inspect the rolls before cutting.

5. Murals can waste less—but still need allowance

A made-to-measure mural is commonly supplied as numbered panels forming one larger image, so there is less waste from repeating a motif than with repeating wallpaper. There can still be trimming, panel alignment, uneven walls, and damaged or unusable sections to consider.

Follow that mural supplier’s exact measuring and ordering instructions. Some add a fit margin themselves; adding another allowance without checking could make the mural too large.

6. Get the wall ready

  • Start with a surface that is clean, dry, smooth, and sound.
  • Address loose paint, old adhesive, holes, rough spots, and active moisture before hanging.
  • Use primer or sizing only as appropriate for the wall and wallpaper system.
  • Let new paint and repairs cure as long as the wallpaper and adhesive instructions require.
  • Peel-and-stick products can be especially sensitive to texture, dust, humidity, and uncured paint.

7. Plan the install before the first strip

  1. Inspect the paper, pattern, color, and lot numbers before cutting.
  2. Choose a starting point and plan where seams, corners, and focal patterns will land.
  3. Set a straight vertical reference; do not assume a room corner is perfectly plumb.
  4. Apply adhesive or peel the backing only as directed, in a section you can hang while workable.
  5. Align the strip and match the pattern, smooth gently, trim with a sharp blade, and clean up only as the product allows.
  6. Allow the installation to dry or cure under the conditions specified by the product makers.

What has worked for me

Rob’s field-tested wallpaper tips

  • Cutting allowance: I leave about 2 inches at the top and bottom, and 1½–2 inches at side corners—not every strip seam—for fitting. The exact cut depends on the wall and product.
  • Paste-the-wall adhesive: I often use Roman PRO-880 for a broad range of papers. I roll it on with a ⅜-inch nap roller, slightly past the strip edges, and usually paste one strip-width at a time.
  • More working time: When the product and wall allow it, I may use PRO-543 and prepare up to two strip-widths at a time. That is my workflow preference—not a rule for every paper or surface.
  • Straight, unmarked guide: I prefer a laser level over drawing a line on the wall, especially behind light-colored wallpaper where marks might show through.
  • Pattern alignment: I secure the top temporarily with low-tack masking tape if it will not damage the paper, align the edge against the previous strip first, and check the pattern around eye level before smoothing. If the pattern cannot align perfectly top to bottom, I prioritize the eye-level match rather than stretching the paper to force it.
  • Clean finish: I keep my hands clean, use a snap-off blade with a fresh edge, and gently dab excess paste with a clean, damp microfiber cloth only if the wallpaper allows it. I avoid rubbing because some inks or finishes can transfer.
Product note: Roman describes PRO-880 for most wallpaper types and weights; PRO-543 is intended for lightweight to medium-weight wallcoverings on sealed, painted surfaces and offers extended open time and slip. I check the wallpaper maker’s instructions and wall conditions before choosing. Dry time also varies with application and the room.

Common questions

Can you tell me how many rolls to buy?

Send the wall dimensions and the wallpaper product link or a photo of its label. Width, length, repeat, match type, and package size all affect the estimate.

Should I subtract doors and windows?

Record them, but do not subtract their area blindly. Their location may not reduce the number of full strips or the material needed to keep a pattern aligned.

Can you help choose and calculate the material?

Yes. You can provide the wallpaper or share the look you want; include your wall measurements and I can help review the product and estimate what to order.

Do you install wallpaper in Salt Lake City and Salt Lake County?

Yes. I take wallpaper installation requests across Salt Lake City and Salt Lake County, including accent walls, nurseries, murals, and room refreshes. Share the project city and wall details so I can confirm the scope.

How much does wallpaper installation cost?

Cost depends on wall dimensions, wallpaper type and pattern, wall condition, existing wallpaper, and access around corners or fixtures. Send the product link, measurements, and photos so I can review your specific project and quote the scope.

Can wallpaper go over textured walls?

It depends on the texture and wallpaper. Texture may show through or affect adhesion, especially with peel-and-stick paper. Send close-up photos and the product details so I can review surface preparation before confirming the installation.

How long does wallpaper installation take?

Timing depends on the number and size of the walls, pattern matching, preparation or removal, and access. I confirm the schedule after reviewing the wall condition and wallpaper product; drying time may also affect the project.
